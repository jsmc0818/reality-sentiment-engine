REFERENCE SCENARIO
Inspect the cash-flow model

FCFF = revenue × operating margin × (1 − tax) − net reinvestment. Net reinvestment = positive revenue change ÷ sales-to-capital. Terminal cash flow uses terminal growth and year-five margin. Equity value = discounted FCFF + cash − debt, floored at zero.

Five-year model; no separate option value, acquisitions or future share issuance. Stock compensation remains an expense in operating profit. Cash and reported debt are simplified proxies. Terminal assumptions can dominate the result.

Why this project exists ↗
01 What does market mood measure?

A signed price-pressure proxy, not direct observation of emotion: 30% three-month return, 25% return relative to SPY, 25% distance from the 200-day average, and 20% signed volume balance over 20 sessions. Returns and trend use ±25% scales; relative returns use ±15 points, all clipped to ±1. Weighted components map to 0–100. Below 30 means selling pressure; above 70 means buying pressure. No claim that low volatility equals greed.

02 What counts as resilient?

Nonnegative latest-quarter year-over-year revenue growth, margin compression no worse than one percentage point, positive trailing free cash flow, and net debt no greater than three times operating cash flow. Sales contraction beyond 5%, margin compression beyond 3 points, nonpositive operating profit or net debt beyond four times positive operating cash flow flags weakness. Otherwise, mixed. These are unvalidated screening rules, not a moat rating. EPS revisions remain separate expectations evidence.

03 How are reference values built?

Defaults are mechanical starting assumptions: latest-quarter revenue growth clipped to 0–25%, TTM operating margin clipped to 1–65% (both rounded to half points), 10% discount rate, 3% terminal growth, 21% tax and a 2× sales-to-capital ratio. They are not independent forecasts or analyst-approved fair values. Bear: growth −5 points, margin −3 points, discount rate +2 points. Bull: growth +5 points, margin +3 points, discount rate −1 point, subject to control limits. Scenarios are not confidence intervals.

04 What changes the research label?

A discount requires price at least 20% below reference value. A premium requires price more than 15% above reference value. Fear discount candidates also require mood ≤30 and resilient financial evidence. Optimism priced in requires mood ≥70 and a premium. Other discounts, premiums, neutral and unclear states remain visible. Model value relative to price is not proof of mispricing.

05 What is measured, assumed or missing?

Prices and financial statements are sourced through Yahoo Finance. Fiscal periods, retrieval times, raw quarter values and estimate target dates are retained. Retrieved-today does not mean every estimate was updated today. No invented news or causal claim explains a selloff. Company questions are research prompts. Stale or incomplete evidence blocks classification. Weekday freshness is conservative around holidays.

06 Has this beaten a fear-only index?

No. This methodology starts prospectively with its first stock observation. Daily underlying observations are archived for later evaluation. Earlier index episodes and proxy backtests test different methodologies and cannot validate this stock framework. Evaluate all qualifying setups against fear alone and simple accumulation policies, including failures and missed opportunities.
